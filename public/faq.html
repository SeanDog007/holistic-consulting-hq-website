The Residency Model
4 questions

A residency is a structured, supervised training program completed after formal education — where you apply your knowledge in real-world settings under expert mentorship. Every other clinical profession (medicine, nursing, pharmacy) uses this model because it works. You don't truly learn how to practice by sitting through more lectures.

"School teaches you what to know. A residency teaches you how to practice."

At HCI, that means supervised case work, live mentoring, real client systems, business tools, and professional network building — not just another course to add to your wall.

HCI is not a degree-granting institution, and our programs are not accredited in the traditional academic sense. What we provide is professional development training that directly supports your licensure, credentialing, and clinical practice goals.

The GROW program is specifically structured to help you accumulate the professional contact hours and clinical preparation required for the BCHN exam through NANP. Details on how HCI training maps to your specific credential requirements will be confirmed on your discovery call.

Supervision and mentoring varies by program stage. In GROW, you have access to live mentoring sessions three times per week, where you bring real case work, clinical questions, business challenges, and career goals to the table. Sessions are led by experienced practitioners and are focused on application — not theory.

You're not passively watching. You're actively working through your practice, with expert eyes on your progress every step of the way. Full details for each program stage are covered in your discovery call.

Most nutrition mentorship programs focus on one thing: clinical skills, or business, or exam prep. HCI trains the whole practitioner — clinical competence, business systems, professional credentialing, community, and career positioning — in one structured residency experience.

We also have a genuine track record. Our graduates are landing jobs in the field, earning their BCHN, building full client caseloads, and advancing into specializations. Those aren't promises — they're stories you can read on our Results page.

Credentials & Certification
4 questions

The BCHN (Board Certified in Holistic Nutrition®) is the premier professional credential for holistic nutrition practitioners, awarded by the National Association of Nutrition Professionals (NANP). It demonstrates advanced clinical knowledge and professional standing in the field.

The GROW program includes structured BCHN exam preparation — covering the clinical knowledge tested on the exam — along with support in accumulating the required professional contact hours through NANP. Multiple HCI graduates have passed the BCHN on their first attempt.

HCI provides completion recognition for practitioners who finish their residency stage. Our programs are professional development training, not degree programs — but they are built to directly support your credentialing goals (BCHN, professional contact hours, clinical case documentation) through the relevant professional bodies.

Specific completion documentation and how it applies to your credential pathway will be confirmed during your discovery call.

Yes — working toward your BCHN is one of the core pathways within GROW. Many practitioners join specifically for the BCHN preparation alongside the clinical training and business support. If you hold or are working toward a qualifying credential (NTP, CHC, CNS, or equivalent), you're likely a strong fit.

NANP eligibility requirements apply — your discovery call will help clarify whether you meet the prerequisites and how GROW supports your specific path to the BCHN.

HCI has a strong relationship with NANP (National Association of Nutrition Professionals) and aligns its GROW program with NANP's professional development and contact hour requirements. Our faculty hold professional credentials including BCHN and clinical herbalism certifications.

Full faculty credentials and HCI affiliations are available on request and will be shared during your discovery call.
